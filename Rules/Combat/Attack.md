Attacking is two steps: land the hit, then roll the damage.

## 1. Land the Hit
Make a normal contested skill check (see [[Skill Checks]]) using your attack skill. The defender chooses what skill to defend with — usually [[Armor|Defend]] if they're wearing armor that grants it, otherwise whatever else applies ([[Do Something]], Acrobatics, etc.). See [[Difficulty]] for how the AI sizes an NPC's side of the roll.

In practice, a crawler rolls their own side and the AI rolls or narrates the opposition directly — an enemy doesn't need its own full character sheet for this to work. Using a weapon skill may cost Stamina; see [[Effort]]. Whether the attack is even possible, and how hard it is, may depend on distance — see [[Range]].

If you win the contest, the attack hits. A loss or a tie means it doesn't — no damage.

## 2. Roll Damage
On a hit, roll the weapon's damage separately: its own dice (e.g. 1d8, set per weapon) + the relevant ability modifier (usually STR) + half your character Level, rounded up.

The number of damage dice matches the dice count you rolled the hit at, not just the weapon's base — a weapon's own dice count is a floor, not a fixed value: attacking with a bigger pool (a higher-level combat skill, rolled at its full level) rolls that many damage dice instead, never fewer than the weapon's own base even at the lowest roll.

This is otherwise a flat roll, not a contest — nothing about the hit roll's *margin* carries over into it, only the dice count. A better weapon or a higher-level character hits harder regardless of how narrowly the hit landed.

## Unarmed Strikes
No weapon equipped? Every crawler has a baseline Unarmed Strike that works exactly like a weapon for the damage step — its own damage dice + the relevant ability modifier + half your Level, rounded up (default: 1d4 + STR modifier + half Level). Items, feats, and classes can upgrade this baseline the same way they'd grant or improve any other weapon.

The hit step still just uses whatever combat skill applies — [[Do Something]] if you don't have anything better.

## Example
Johnny Rotten attacks with Slash (a Battleaxe skill, rolled at its full 3d6). His Slash roll beats the AI's contest — the attack hits.
He then rolls the Battleaxe's damage: 3d8 (matching the 3 dice he rolled Slash at) + his STR modifier + half his Level, rounded up.

If this damage reduces an Individual Threat to 0 HP or below, see [[Dying]]. An Extra is simply removed from the fight at 0 HP.
